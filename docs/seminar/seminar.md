# Seminar Summary: Hic1+ Mesenchymal Progenitors in Tissue Generation and Regeneration

**PI:** Scott Wilder (wilder.scott@sri.utoronto.ca), Sunnybrook Research Institute / University of Toronto  
**Closing line:** *"Behind Every Great Stem Cell is a Mesenchymal Progenitor"*

---

## Overview

This seminar presents work characterising **Hic1** (Hairy/Enhancer of Split Related with YRPW Motif 1) as a definitive marker for **Mesenchymal Progenitor/Stem Cells (MPs/MSCs)** across development and adult tissue homeostasis and repair. The talk is divided into two major themes: **Tissue Generation** (embryonic lineage tracing) and **Tissue Regeneration** (injury response across multiple organs).

---

## Part 1 — The Mesenchymal Progenitor Identity Problem

- The connective tissue field suffers from a proliferation of names for what may be the same cell: MSCs, BMSCs, stromal cells, fibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs), pericytes, etc. (p01)
- **Hic1 expression is restricted to mesenchymal progenitors** and is lost upon differentiation (e.g., Hic1+ progenitors → Hic1− chondrocytes in micromass culture; validated by SOX9 immunostaining). (p02)

### The Genetic Tool: Hic1^CreERT2 / Rosa26^SL-tdTomato

- A tamoxifen-inducible Cre system (*Hic1^CreERT2*) combined with a Rosa26 Cre-reporter (tdTomato) permanently labels Hic1+ cells and all their progeny at the time of tamoxifen injection. (p03–p04)
- Enables **identification, isolation, and functional manipulation** of MPs/MSCs *in vivo* at any developmental or adult timepoint.

---

## Part 2 — Tissue Generation (Embryonic Development)

### Forelimb Lineage Tracing

- Hic1-labelled cells in the E11.5 limb bud are a **distinct mesenchymal population**, clearly separate from Neural Crest, Lateral Plate Mesoderm (LPM), and myogenic progenitors by scRNA-seq. (p07)
- Marker genes: *Hic1*, *Lhx9* for MPs; *Myf5/MyoD1/Pax7* for myogenic; *Krt5/Krt14* for epithelium.

### Lineage Trees — Computational + In Vivo Validation

- Single-cell lineage trees map **differentiation trajectories** from embryonic Hic1+ MPs into: Fibrogenic Progenitors, Fibroblasts, Mesenchymal Progenitors I & II, Chondrocytes, Dermal Progenitors, Pericytes. (p08)
- Computationally-inferred lineages are validated by in vivo immunofluorescence. (p09)
- A **rare mesenchymal subset** is enriched specifically at musculoskeletal **attachment sites** (entheses/myotendinous junctions) and has a propensity to remodel those sites. (p10)

### Broad Connective Tissue Lineage Potential

- Embryonic Hic1+ MPs contribute to: BMSCs, Cartilage, Dense Fascia, Endofascia, Fascia, Fibroblasts, Pericytes, Provisional Callus, SMCs, Tendon, White Adipose Tissue — each with lineage-specific transcription factors. (p11)
- Some progeny integrate into connective tissues; others **persist into adulthood** retaining Hic1 expression and specialised functions. (p12)

## Part 3 — Adult Hic1+ MSCs at Homeostasis

- Hic1+ MSCs are present in **all vascularised tissues** examined: spleen, brain, liver, renal cortex, renal medulla, pancreas, WAT, BAT, lung, myocardium, tibialis anterior muscle. (p13)
- They reside in **perivascular and peri-epithelial stromal compartments**.
- Adult Hic1+ MSCs are **quiescent** — population size does not change over 1+ year in uninjured animals. (p14)
- Hic1+ cells contribute to the **white adipocyte lineage** in adipose tissue over time (D0 → D30 → D60 → D180). (p15)
- Lineage tracing (Enpp1^CreERT2) shows MSC contribution to **osteoblasts and bone remodelling** at homeostasis in the cortical bone and bone marrow. (p16–p17)

### Mesenchymal Progenitor Atlas — 13 Major Organs

- FACS-sorted Hic1+ cells from 13 organs were profiled by **scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq**. (p18)
- Two major populations identified across organs: **FAP** (fibro-adipogenic progenitor) and **Pericytic** subsets. (p19)
- Stellate cells, previously thought tissue-specific, cluster with the broader MSC atlas.
- Human conservation confirmed in **Tabula Sapiens (1.3M cells)**: PDGFRA, COL1A1, PRRX1, and HIC1 co-mark the equivalent human stromal population. (p20)
- MSC subsets show both **pan-tissue conserved** subpopulations and **tissue-specific** populations (e.g., Ptf10+, Pax7+, Pecam2+). (p21)

## Part 4 — Tissue Regeneration (Injury Response)

### Hic1+ MSCs Activate Across Multiple Injury Models

| Organ | Injury Model | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Pancreas | Caerulein (secretagogue) | Hic1+ cells expand D0–D14 post injury (p23) |
| Skin | 6 mm biopsy punch wound | Distinct activated states: small wound, large wound, LW centre at D14 (p24) |
| Liver | CCl4 acute injury | Hepatic stellate cell–like activation; activated vs quiescent subclusters (p25) |
| Heart | LAD ligation (MI) | Fibroblast activation at 7 DPI; pericyte and activated FB clusters (p26) |
| Brain | Photothrombotic stroke | MSC proliferation and directed migration toward lesion D1–D21; spatially resolved by distance from infarct (p27–p28) |
| Bone | Bone fracture defect | Contribution to clot formation → callus → ossification → remodelling (D7–D28) (p29) |
| Tendon | Patellar defect / NTX | Hic1+ MSCs contribute to tenocytes, myotenocytes, and restored tendon tissue (p38–p43) |

### MSC Activation Cycle

A universal **stage-specific activation programme** is observed across injury contexts: (p30–p34)

1. **Quiescence** (homeostasis)
2. **Cytokine response** (early inflammatory signalling)
3. **Cell expansion** (proliferation; cell cycle gene upregulation)
4. **Provisional matrix deposition** (fibronectin, collagens; tissue scaffolding)
5. **Return to quiescence** (basement membrane gene expression; niche re-establishment)

Late in bone/muscle repair, a tenogenic/MTJ programme is also hinted at within the activation cycle.

### Muscle & Tendon Focus

- scRNA-seq of muscle MSCs confirms a **heterogeneous population** that dynamically shifts composition from D0 → D14 post injury. (p35)
- A **Scx (Scleraxis)+** tenogenic cluster emerges prominently by D14. (p36)
- scATAC-seq confirms **lineage-restricted chromatin accessibility** in MP subsets (Alpha → Zeta), each with distinct open chromatin at tissue-specific loci. (p37)
- **Hic1+ MPs give rise to Scx-GFP+ tenocytes and myotenocytes** after cardiotoxin (NTX) injury: ~11.3% of Scx-GFP+ cells are tdTomato+ at D28 post-NTX (vs. 7.4% uninjured). (p38)
- A dedicated **myotenocyte progenitor** (Hic1+) regenerates the myotendinous junction (MTJ): absent at 0 DPI, present at 14 DPI. (p39)
- In the **Patellar Tendon Defect Model** (4-month healing): tdTomato+ MSC-derived cells significantly enriched in the injured vs uninjured tendon. (p40–p41)
- MSCs contribute to **multiple cell fates during tendon healing**: tenocytes and other stromal cells; active gene programs are under investigation. (p42)
- Time-course of **Achilles tendon healing** (Hic1^tdTomato / Scx-GFP dual reporter): MSC-derived tdTomato+ cells accumulate from day 1 through 4 weeks post injury. (p43)

### Restoring Neonatal Regenerative Capacity

- Neonates (E12.5–E15.5 tendon formation window) show **superior regenerative capacity** compared to adults.
- Transplanting neonatal MSCs into injured adult tendons partially **restores regeneration**, suggesting the cell-intrinsic state of the MSC, not just the environment, determines repair quality. (p44)

---

## Summary

- MSCs are activated in response to injury in **every major organ tested** (skin, liver, pancreas, bone, heart, brain, tendon). (p45)
- Hic1 provides a unified, specific marker to track these cells from embryo through adulthood.
- A shared **MSC activation cycle** (quiescence → cytokines → proliferation → provisional matrix → quiescence) underlies repair across tissues.
- In the musculoskeletal context, Hic1+ MPs give rise specifically to **tenocytes and myotenocytes** and are essential for tendon regeneration.
- Future directions include understanding the transcriptional programs driving tenogenic fate, and leveraging neonatal MSC biology to improve adult tendon repair.
