### Scott et al. (2019) — Hic1 Defines Quiescent Mesenchymal Progenitor Subpopulations with Distinct Functions and Fates in Skeletal Muscle Regeneration

**Journal:** Cell Stem Cell  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 25, Issue 6, pp. 797–813.e9  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.stem.2019.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2019.11.004)  
**Authors:** Scott RW, Arostegui M, Schweitzer R, Rossi FMV, Underhill TM

**Summary:**  
This foundational study establishes Hic1 as a marker of quiescent mesenchymal progenitors (MPs) in adult skeletal muscle. Using scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq, the study identifies four Hic1+ subpopulations: two fibroadipogenic progenitor (FAP) clusters — FAP1 (Dpt+, Col15a1+, enriched for ECM genes including Col4a1, Col6 transcripts, Bgn, Mgp) and FAP2 (Dpt+, Pi16+, enriched for trophic/signaling genes including Sfrp4, Wnt2, Sema3c, Tgfbr2) — a pericytic population (Rgs5+, Pdgfrb+, Kcnj8+, Abcc9+, laminin-encased), and a tenogenic population (Scx+) that contributes Col22a1-expressing myotenocytes at the myotendinous junction (MTJ). All four populations exhibit open chromatin regions (OCRs) across cell-cycle loci and enter the cell cycle upon injury-induced activation, confirming progenitor status. Hic1+ MP subsets generate enduring progeny including tenocytes, myotenocytes and epimysial fibroblasts. Conditional Hic1 deletion leads to MP hyperplasia, demonstrating that Hic1 is required to maintain quiescence. The FAP1/FAP2 subsets correspond to the universal fibroblast profiles Dpt+ Col15a1+ and Dpt+ Pi16+ found in mouse and human tissues.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; quiescent mesenchymal progenitors; FAP1/FAP2; pericytes; myotenocytes; Col22a1; MTJ; scRNA-seq; scATAC-seq; lineage tracing; skeletal muscle

### Soliman et al. (2020) — Pathogenic Potential of Hic1-Expressing Cardiac Stromal Progenitors

**Journal:** Cell Stem Cell  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 26, Issue 2, pp. 205–220.e8  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.stem.2019.12.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2019.12.008)  
**Authors:** Soliman H, Paylor B, Scott RW, Lemos DR, Chang C, Arostegui M, Low M, Lee C, Fiore D, Braghetta P, Pospichalova V, Barkauskas CE, Korinek V, Rampazzo A, MacLeod K, Underhill TM, Rossi FMV

**Summary:**  
This paper extends the Hic1+ progenitor framework to the heart. Cardiac fibroadipogenic progenitors (cFAPs) are identified as PDGFRα+, SCA-1+ and Hic1+ cells residing quiescently in the cardiac interstitium. Upon injury or in disease contexts, SCA-1+ progenitors give rise to SCA-1− fibrogenic progeny that produce pathological fibrofatty tissue. To model arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (AC), the study uses a transgenic DSG2 mouse (overexpressing desmoglein-2), which develops AC-like fibrofatty infiltrations. Hic1-CreERT2 lineage tracing directly demonstrates that Hic1+ cFAPs are the cellular source of these lesions. Conditional deletion of Hic1 in cardiac progenitors produces an AC-like phenotype, establishing Hic1 as a regulator of cardiac progenitor homeostasis. Pharmacological inhibition with nilotinib (BCR-ABL/PDGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor) blocks cFAP differentiation into fibrogenic progeny, identifying PDGF signaling as a potential therapeutic target in AC.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; cardiac fibroadipogenic progenitors (cFAPs); arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy; fibrofatty infiltration; DSG2 model; SCA-1; PDGFRα; nilotinib; lineage tracing

### Abbasi et al. (2020) — Distinct Regulatory Programs Control the Latent Regenerative Potential of Dermal Fibroblasts during Wound Healing

**Journal:** Cell Stem Cell  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 27, Issue 3, pp. 396–412.e6  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.stem.2020.07.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2020.07.008)  
**Authors:** Abbasi S, Sinha S, Labit E, Rosin NL, Yoon G, Rahmani W, Jaffer A, Sharma N, Hagner A, Shah P, Arora R, Yoon J, Islam A, Uchida A, Chang CK, Stratton JA, Scott RW, Rossi FMV, Underhill TM, Biernaskie J

**Summary:**  
This study identifies the cellular and regulatory basis of wound-induced hair follicle neogenesis (WIHN) in murine skin. The central finding is that extrafollicular Hic1+ dermal progenitors — not hair follicle dermal sheath cells (hfDSCs) — are the primary source (>90%) of neogenic dermal papilla (DP) cells in large wounds. hfDSCs make only minor contributions to neogenic DP. Runx1 and retinoic acid (RA) signaling are established as master regulatory axes governing whether Hic1+ progenitor activation leads to regenerative (hair follicle-forming) or fibrotic outcomes. Conditional deletion of Hic1 in dermal progenitors increases WIHN approximately 4-fold, indicating that Hic1 actively suppresses regenerative output. Single-cell analyses reveal heterogeneity within the Hic1+ dermal MP compartment, with distinct transcriptional states corresponding to pro-regenerative and pro-fibrotic programs. This work establishes the extrafollicular niche as the key reservoir supporting skin regeneration and frames Hic1 as a rheostat controlling the latent regenerative capacity of adult dermis.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; dermal mesenchymal progenitors; wound-induced hair follicle neogenesis (WIHN); dermal papilla; Runx1; retinoic acid; hfDSCs; extrafollicular progenitors; skin regeneration; fibrosis

### Arostegui et al. (2022) — Cellular Taxonomy of Hic1+ Mesenchymal Progenitor Derivatives in the Limb: From Embryo to Adult

**Journal:** Nature Communications  
**Volume/Pages:** 13:4989  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-022-32695-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32695-1)  
**Authors:** Arostegui M, Scott RW, Böse K, Underhill TM

**Summary:**  
This study resolves the embryonic origin and complete fate map of Hic1+ MPs in the mouse appendicular skeleton using time-resolved scRNA-seq and inducible lineage tracing (Hic1CreERT2; R26tdTomato with TAM pulse at E10). Quantitative PCR and whole-mount imaging show that Hic1+ MPs are absent from the limb at E10.5 and first appear at E11.5, migrating from the sclerotome and syndetome compartments of the hypaxial somite — explicitly not from the dermomyotome or neural crest. By E14.5, Hic1-lineage cells comprise ~26.6% of limb mesenchymal cells. scRNA-seq at E11.5 shows tdTomato+ cells cluster separately from LPM-derived mesenchyme and co-express Hic1 and Aldh1a2 (retinoic acid synthesis gene). Fate-mapping reveals that Hic1+ MPs generate the following adult populations: FAP1 (Dpt+, Col15a1+), FAP2 (Dpt+, Pi16+), pericytes (Rgs5+, Pdgfrb+, Kcnj8+, Abcc9+), tenocytes, myotenocytes (Col22a1+), fascia-resident fibroblasts, peri- and endoneurial cells, and bone marrow stromal cells. Contributions to chondrocytes and osteocytes within the primary skeletal elements are limited. All adult progenitor compartments maintain Hic1 expression and re-enter the cell cycle upon injury. The study establishes a comprehensive developmental cellular taxonomy linking embryonic somite-derived MPs to the full spectrum of adult connective tissue cell types in the limb.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; limb mesenchymal progenitors; sclerotome; syndetome; embryonic origin; fate mapping; FAP1/FAP2; pericytes; myotenocytes; tenocytes; perineurial cells; scRNA-seq; lineage tracing

### Arostegui et al. (2023) — Hic1 Identifies a Specialized Mesenchymal Progenitor Population in the Embryonic Limb Responsible for Bone Superstructure Formation

**Journal:** Cell Reports  
**Volume/Pages:** 42, 112325  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.celrep.2023.112325](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.112325)  
**Authors:** Arostegui M, Scott RW, Underhill TM

**Summary:**  
This study shows that Hic1 identifies a subset of Scx+/Sox9+ tenochondroprogenitors in the embryonic limb bud that are responsible for a secondary wave of bone morphogenesis — the formation of bone superstructures including ridges, protuberances, and eminences at muscle-tendon-bone interfaces. Using Hic1CreERT2; R26tdTomato; ScxGFP triple-allele lineage tracing, the study demonstrates that Hic1-lineage MPs contribute specifically to the entheses (tendon-to-bone attachment sites), myotendinous junctions (MTJs), and associated skeletal superstructures (medial epicondyle, olecranon, radial tuberosity, greater tubercle), but explicitly not to the primary cartilaginous anlagen of the long bones nor to the tendon bodies — consistent with the "modular model" of bone morphogenesis in which Scx+/Sox9+ progenitors add eminences onto a pre-formed skeletal template. scRNA-seq of FAC-sorted tdTomato+ cells at E11.5 and E12.5 (32,225 cells total) reveals that chondrogenic and tenogenic markers overlap broadly in early Hic1+ MPs, and pseudotime analysis shows Sox9 expression diverges as cells commit to chondrogenic versus tenogenic fates. To assess functional consequences, conditional deletion of Hic1 using Prrx1-Cre; Hic1f/f (achieving ~92% deletion efficiency at E13.5) produces mice that are viable but display severe skeletal superstructure defects: the ulnar tuberosity is absent, the olecranon is thinner and shorter, the radial tuberosity is absent, and radial/ulnar ridges are absent. The resulting bones show an overall featureless cylindrical morphology, with disorganized enthesis fibrocartilage and disturbed unmineralized-mineralized continuum.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; tenochondroprogenitors; Scx; Sox9; bone superstructures; entheses; myotendinous junctions; secondary bone morphogenesis; Prrx1-Cre; Hic1f/f; conditional knockout; scRNA-seq; lineage tracing

### Hill et al. (2025) — The Fibroblast Epigenome Underlies SS18::SSX-Mediated Transformation in Synovial Sarcoma

**Journal:** Nature Communications  
**Volume/Pages:** 16:10830  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-025-65850-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65850-5)  
**Authors:** Hill LA, Scott RW, Martin LA, Arostegui M, Davenport G, Vemon M, Hofvander J, Wang XQ, Li J, Nielsen TO, Jones KB, Hirst M, Underhill TM

**Summary:**  
This study defines the cell-of-origin and epigenomic mechanism of synovial sarcoma (SyS), an aggressive soft-tissue malignancy caused by a t(X;18)(p11.2;q11.2) translocation producing the SS18::SSX fusion oncogene. A Hic1CreERT2 line was used to express a conditional SS18::SSX2 allele (RosahSS2) in Hic1+ mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs), producing SyS at 100% penetrance with a median latency of 16.2 ± 2.8 weeks post-tamoxifen. Tumours arose at characteristic anatomical sites (tongue, stifle/knee, tail base, trunk) and recapitulated human SyS histologically (monophasic, biphasic, and poorly differentiated subtypes) and molecularly (TLE1+, SS18::SSX+, EMA1+; neural/developmental transcriptional signature). Genetic refinement of the cell-of-origin identified a rare Hic1+ Pdgfra+ Lgr5+ fibroblastic subpopulation as the cell most susceptible to SS18::SSX2-mediated transformation. Epigenomic analysis revealed that adult and embryonic MSCs share overlapping H2AK119ub (PRC1) and H3K4me3/H3K27me3 bivalent (PRC2) histone marks. SS18::SSX2 disrupts BAF chromatin remodeling complexes (preferentially degrading CBAF while perturbing GBAF/PBAF stoichiometry) and causes widespread loss of H3K27me3 at bivalently-marked loci, de-repressing an embryonic MSC transcriptional program. The study thus links the oncogenic mechanism (BAF/PRC disruption) to a specific cellular context (Hic1+ Pdgfra+ Lgr5+ fibroblasts) whose epigenome is uniquely permissive to SS18::SSX-induced transformation.

**Key concepts:** synovial sarcoma; SS18::SSX2; Hic1+ MSCs; Pdgfra+; Lgr5+; BAF complexes; PRC1/PRC2; H3K27me3; H2AK119ub; bivalent marks; fibroblast epigenome; cell-of-origin; lineage tracing

### Bernier et al. (2025) — Brain Pericytes and Perivascular Fibroblasts Are Stromal Progenitors with Dual Functions in Cerebrovascular Regeneration after Stroke

**Journal:** Nature Neuroscience  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 28, pp. 517–535  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41593-025-01872-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01872-y)  
**Authors:** Bernier LP, Hefendehl JK, Scott RW, Tung LW, Lewis CA, Soliman H, Simm S, Dissing-Olesen L, Hofmann J, Guo D, DeMeglio M, Rossi FM, Underhill TM, MacVicar BA

**Summary:**  
This study identifies the Hic1+ stromal progenitor cell (SPC) populations of the adult brain and characterizes their coordinated responses to ischemic stroke. Using Hic1nLacZ reporter mice, Hic1-expressing cells are found exclusively in the perivascular stromal compartment, embedded within the laminin+ basement membrane of cerebral vessels. Photothrombotic stroke (cortical hindlimb somatosensory cortex) is used as an injury model, and longitudinal in vivo OCT angiography documents remarkable functional revascularization over 21 days. Fate-tracking of Hic1+ SPCs reveals three subpopulations in the perivascular niche: pericytes (most abundant; angiogenic profile post-stroke), venular smooth muscle cells (SMCs; intermediate), and perivascular fibroblasts (fibrogenic profile post-stroke). scRNA-seq analysis characterizes the transcriptional activation states of each population after stroke, showing that pericytes undergo a transient angiogenic program while perivascular fibroblasts drive a distinct fibrogenic program — the two populations remain segregated and do not interconvert. Both pericytes and perivascular fibroblasts expand in the infarct region where they associate with endothelial cells inside a reactive astrocyte border. The study establishes pericytes and perivascular fibroblasts as the brain's SPC equivalents of the FAP and pericyte compartments described in peripheral tissues, and demonstrates that their coordinated but distinct activities enable cerebrovascular regeneration after stroke.

**Key concepts:** Hic1; brain stromal progenitors; pericytes; perivascular fibroblasts; venular SMCs; photothrombotic stroke; cerebrovascular regeneration; blood-brain barrier; angiogenesis; scRNA-seq; lineage tracing

### Howell et al. (2017) — Novel Model of Tendon Regeneration Reveals Distinct Cell Mechanisms Underlying Regenerative and Fibrotic Tendon Healing

**Journal:** Scientific Reports  
**Volume/Pages:** 7:45238  
**DOI:** [10.1038/srep45238](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep45238)  
**Authors:** Howell K, Chien C, Bell R, Laudier D, Tufa SF, Keene DR, Andarawis-Puri N, Huang AH

**Summary:**  
This paper establishes a neonatal mouse model of tendon regeneration using a full-thickness patellar tendon defect and contrasts it with adult healing to uncover fundamentally distinct cellular mechanisms. In neonatal mice, full-thickness defects heal via formation of a neo-tendon that differentiates along the tendon-specific lineage, with restoration of gait and mechanical properties. In contrast, adults heal via fibrovascular scar, aberrant differentiation toward cartilage and bone, and persistently impaired function. Genetic lineage tracing using a constitutive Scx-Cre driver reveals that intrinsic recruitment of Scx-lineage tenocytes into the defect site is a key cellular mechanism of neonatal regeneration that is absent in adult healing. In adults, Scx-lineage tenocytes are not recruited into the defect; instead, they transdifferentiate into ectopic cartilage at the wound margins. In the absence of tenogenic cells, extrinsic SMA-expressing cells (myofibroblasts/mesenchymal progenitors) persist in the defect and form a permanent fibrotic scar. The study establishes this neonatal patellar tendon model as a useful tool for investigating the molecular basis of tendon regeneration, and identifies intrinsic Scx-lineage cell recruitment as a hallmark that distinguishes regenerative from non-regenerative tendon healing.

**Key concepts:** tendon regeneration; neonatal model; patellar tendon; full-thickness defect; Scx-Cre; lineage tracing; Scx-lineage tenocytes; SMA-expressing cells; fibrotic scar; neo-tendon; regenerative vs fibrotic healing

### Harvey et al. (2019) — A Tppp3+Pdgfra+ Tendon Stem Cell Population Contributes to Regeneration and Reveals a Shared Role for PDGF Signalling in Regeneration and Fibrosis

**Journal:** Nature Cell Biology  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 21, pp. 1490–1503  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41556-019-0417-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-019-0417-z)  
**Authors:** Harvey T, Flamenco S, Fan CM

**Summary:**  
This study uses a regeneration-competent patellar tendon punch-injury model (biopsy punch injury, not Achilles tendon transection) to identify bona fide tendon stem cells in adult mice. Single-cell RNA-seq of 2,491 cells from whole adult patellar tendons defines 8 clusters, including tenocytes (cluster 4: Fmod+, Tnmd+, Thbs4+), Tppp3+ paratenon sheath cells (cluster 2: Tppp3+, Prg4+), T-FAPs (cluster 3: Tppp3−, Pdgfra+, Ly6a+), and non-stromal cells (RBCs, osteoblasts, endothelial cells, macrophages, APCs). Two custom knock-in alleles are engineered at the Tppp3 locus: Tppp3CG (CreERT2 + eGFP) for dual real-time identification and permanent lineage marking, and Tppp3ECE (ERT2-flanked Cre) combinable with ScxGFP to monitor Scx expression in the Tppp3 lineage. Lineage tracing from P90 adults shows that Tppp3+ sheath cells are largely quiescent (~5.4% EdU+ per month) and do not contribute to the midsubstance under homeostasis, but after patellar tendon punch injury these cells proliferate, infiltrate the defect region, and give rise to tenocyte-like progeny (aligned, FMOD+, TNC+, collagen-SHG+). A fraction of Tppp3+ cells co-expresses Pdgfra (Tppp3+Pdgfra+); exogenous PDGF-AA protein promotes new tenocyte production while conditional inactivation of Pdgfra in Tppp3+ cells blocks tendon regeneration. Critically, Tppp3−Pdgfra+ T-FAPs coexist in the stem cell niche and, upon injury, give rise to fibrotic scar cells rather than tenocytes — revealing that PDGF signaling drives both regeneration (via Tppp3+Pdgfra+ stem cells) and fibrosis (via T-FAPs), explaining why exogenous PDGF application enhances both outcomes simultaneously.

**Key concepts:** Tppp3; tendon stem cells; patellar tendon; punch injury; scRNA-seq; T-FAPs; PDGFRα; PDGF-AA; tendon regeneration; fibrosis; lineage tracing; Tppp3CG; Tppp3ECE

### Kaji et al. (2020) — Tgfβ Signaling Is Required for Tenocyte Recruitment and Functional Neonatal Tendon Regeneration

**Journal:** eLife  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 9, e51779  
**DOI:** [10.7554/eLife.51779](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51779)  
**Authors:** Kaji DA, Howell KL, Balic Z, Hubmacher D, Huang AH

**Summary:**  
Using the neonatal patellar tendon regeneration model established by Howell et al. (2017), this study investigates the requirement for TGFβ signaling in neonatal tendon regeneration and the cellular source of repair tenocytes. The Achilles tendon transection model is employed for functional biomechanical readouts. TGFβ signaling is inhibited using SB-431542, a small-molecule antagonist of ALK4/5/7 (type I TGFβ receptors). In vivo inhibition of TGFβ signaling blocks the migration of tenocytes into the defect zone but does not affect tenocyte proliferation, establishing that TGFβ is required for cell migration rather than cell division during neonatal tendon repair. TGFβ3 is the predominant isoform upregulated at all post-injury timepoints examined. To track the cellular sources of repair, ScxGFP mice are used alongside lineage tracing, revealing that not only Scx-lineage cells but also non-Scx-lineage ScxGFP+ cells contribute to the neotendon. These non-lineage-labeled Scx-GFP+ cells represent a de novo population of tenocyte-committed progenitors recruited to the injury site. Together, the findings establish that TGFβ3 acts as a migratory cue for multiple tenocyte populations during neonatal tendon regeneration and that functional repair depends on this chemotactic activity.

**Key concepts:** TGFβ; neonatal tendon regeneration; SB-431542; ALK4/5/7; tenocyte migration; TGFβ3; Scx-lineage; ScxGFP; non-Scx-lineage tenocytes; neotendon

### Moser et al. (2021) — Cell Lineage Tracing and Functional Assessment of Supraspinatus Tendon Healing in an Acute Repair Murine Model

**Journal:** Journal of Orthopaedic Research  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 39, Issue 8, pp. 1789–1799  
**DOI:** [10.1002/jor.24769](https://doi.org/10.1002/jor.24769)  
**Authors:** Moser HL, Abraham AC, Howell K, Laudier D, Zumstein MA, Galatz LM, Huang AH

**Summary:**  
This study comprehensively characterizes a murine model of full supraspinatus tendon detachment and surgical repair, assessing pain, gait, biomechanics, bone structure, and cell contributions to healing. Inducible Cre lines are used for lineage tracing one week before injury: ScxCreERT2 (labels tenocytes) and SMACreERT2 (labels myofibroblasts and mesenchymal progenitors), both with R26tdTomato reporter, with tamoxifen administered three days consecutively. Pain is assessed by the mouse grimace scale and is only transiently elevated at 3–6 hours post-surgery, returning to baseline by 24 hours — inconsistent with the prolonged pain seen clinically. Gait analysis shows that %Stance Stride is significantly reduced in the repair limb from day 3 to day 14 (more specific for supraspinatus tendon than paw area, which is confounded by deltoid injury). At day 28, all tensile mechanical properties remain permanently impaired: increased cross-sectional area, decreased maximum force, stiffness, work-to-yield, ultimate stress, Young's modulus, and resilience — consistent with scar formation rather than functional tendon regeneration. MicroCT imaging shows permanent cortical and trabecular bone loss at the humeral head enthesis (cortical porosity increased, trabecular BMD and thickness reduced). Lineage tracing reveals that Scxlin cells are largely absent from the repaired region at day 28 (despite the tendon stub not retracting), indicating progressive tendon degeneration rather than regeneration. In contrast, SMAlin cells strongly contribute to the massive hypercellular scar tissue filling the repair site. The study concludes that healing after supraspinatus repair does not originate from the tendon proper, and that tendon degeneration — not just failed enthesis healing — is a major impediment to rotator cuff repair.

**Key concepts:** rotator cuff; supraspinatus tendon; detachment and repair; ScxCreERT2; SMACreERT2; tendon degeneration; scar formation; SMAlin cells; gait analysis; mouse grimace scale; biomechanics; microCT

### Cherief et al. (2023) — TrkA-Mediated Sensory Innervation of Injured Mouse Tendon Supports Tendon Sheath Progenitor Cell Expansion and Tendon Repair

**Journal:** Science Translational Medicine  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 15, Issue 727, eade4619  
**DOI:** [10.1126/scitranslmed.ade4619](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.ade4619)  
**Authors:** Cherief M, Xu J, Li Z, Tower RJ, Ramesh S, Qin Q, Gomez-Salazar M, Yea JH, Lee S, Negri S, Xu M, Price T, Kendal AR, Fan CM, Clemens TL, Levi B, James AW

**Summary:**  
This study establishes a role for sensory innervation in tendon repair by demonstrating that nerve growth factor (NGF)/TrkA signaling is required for tendon sheath progenitor cell (TSPC) expansion and functional tendon healing after Achilles tendon injury. After Achilles tendon injury, new nerve growth occurs near tendon cells expressing NGF. Conditional deletion of Ngf in either myeloid cells or mesenchymal cells limits both post-injury sensory innervation and tendon repair, establishing that both sources contribute NGF to drive reinnervation. Pharmacological inhibition of TrkA (the high-affinity NGF receptor, tropomyosin receptor kinase A) abrogates tendon healing. Sural nerve transection — which physically blocks the post-injury increase in sensory innervation — also blocks expansion of TSPCs expressing Tppp3 (the same marker as the tendon stem cells identified by Harvey et al.), directly linking nerve-derived signals to progenitor activation. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics of injured tendon reveal that disruption of sensory innervation causes dysregulated inflammatory and TGFβ signaling in the wound environment. Culture of mouse TSPCs with conditioned medium from dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons further supports a direct role for neuronal mediators and TGFβ signaling in TSPC proliferation in vitro. Histologic and transcriptomic analyses of human tendon biopsy samples corroborate the pro-regenerative role of innervation and TGFβ in human tendon. Finally, systemic treatment with a small-molecule partial agonist of TrkA after injury increases neurovascular response, TGFβ signaling, TSPC expansion, and tendon tissue repair, demonstrating translational potential.

**Key concepts:** sensory innervation; NGF; TrkA; tendon sheath progenitor cells (TSPCs); Tppp3; Achilles tendon; tendon repair; TGFβ signaling; single-cell transcriptomics; spatial transcriptomics; dorsal root ganglia; TrkA agonist

### Li et al. (2022) — Emerging Artificial Intelligence Applications in Spatial Transcriptomics Analysis

**Journal:** Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 20, pp. 2895–2908  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.csbj.2022.05.056](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2022.05.056)  
**Authors:** Li Y, Stanojevic S, Garmire LX

**Summary:**  
This mini-review surveys AI (machine learning and deep learning) methods developed for spatial transcriptomics (ST) analysis as of 2022, organized across five application areas: (1) spatially variable gene (SVG) detection, (2) clustering, (3) cell-cell communication analysis, (4) deconvolution, and (5) enhancement and imputation. The paper contextualizes ST within the two major technology categories: NGS-based (Visium, Slide-seq, Stereo-seq, GeoMx), where each capture spot typically contains multiple cells, and image-based (STARmap, MERFISH, seqFISH+), which achieve single-cell or subcellular resolution. For SVG detection, the featured SOMDE method combines self-organizing maps (SOM) with a Gaussian Process model, compressing ST data by clustering neighboring spots into meta-nodes before fitting, achieving 5–50× efficiency gains over methods such as spatialDE and SPARK with comparable results on Visium and Slide-seq datasets. The review covers the technical methodology, advantages, limitations, and benchmark performance of representative methods in each category, providing a user-facing guide for researchers developing AI methods for ST. The work is particularly useful for understanding the computational challenges introduced by the combination of spatial coordinates, histology images, and gene expression matrices that define ST datasets.

**Key concepts:** spatial transcriptomics; AI methods; SVG detection; SOMDE; clustering; deconvolution; enhancement; NGS-based ST; image-based ST; deep learning; machine learning

### Yue et al. (2023) — A Guidebook of Spatial Transcriptomic Technologies, Data Resources and Analysis Approaches

**Journal:** Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal  
**Volume/Pages:** Vol. 21, pp. 940–955  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.csbj.2023.01.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2023.01.016)  
**Authors:** Yue L, Liu F, Hu J, Yang P, Wang Y, Dong J, Shu W, Huang X, Wang S

**Summary:**  
This comprehensive guidebook systematically covers spatial transcriptomics technologies, publicly available data resources, and computational analysis approaches, aimed at helping researchers navigate the rapidly growing field. The paper classifies 22 spatial transcriptomic technologies into four categories based on their technical foundations: (1) ISH-based (seqFISH, MERFISH, seqFISH+, DSP/GeoMx, Split-FISH, EEL FISH, CosMx SMI), typically targeted and expensive with high sensitivity; (2) ISS-based (ISS, FISSEQ, STARmap, HybISS, BOLORAMIS, Xenium), using amplification-based RNA readout within intact tissue; (3) NGS-based (Visium at 55–100 µm resolution, Slide-seq at 10 µm, HDST at 2 µm, DBiT-seq, Slide-seqV2, Stereo-seq at 500 nm), which are unbiased/whole-transcriptome but have lower per-spot resolution; and (4) spatial information reconstruction technologies (tomo-seq, STRP-seq), which are imaging-free but computationally reconstruct spatial coordinates. The authors manually collected 791 ST datasets across 479 articles into an integrated data resource. For computational analysis, 70 approaches are catalogued across tasks including pre-processing, batch-effect correction, dimensionality reduction, clustering, spatial cell-type annotation, SVG detection, gene-pattern analysis, spatial region identification, cell-cell interaction, gene-gene interaction, spatial trajectory, and 3D reconstruction. The paper also discusses the emerging field of spatial multimodal omics and its potential applications in tumor microenvironment research.

**Key concepts:** spatial transcriptomics; ISH-based; ISS-based; NGS-based; Visium; MERFISH; seqFISH; STARmap; data resources; computational analysis; spatial multimodal omics; tumor microenvironment; guidebook

### Yang et al. (2025) — STAIG: Spatial Transcriptomics Analysis via Image-Aided Graph Contrastive Learning for Domain Exploration and Alignment-Free Integration

**Journal:** Nature Communications  
**Volume/Pages:** 16:1067  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-025-56276-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56276-0)  
**Authors:** Yang Y, Cui Y, Zeng X, Zhang Y, Loza M, Park SJ, Nakai K

**Summary:**  
This paper presents STAIG (Spatial Transcriptomics Analysis via Image-Aided Graph Contrastive Learning), a deep learning framework for spatial domain identification and multi-slice integration. STAIG integrates three modalities: gene expression, spatial coordinates, and histological (H&E-stained) images. For image feature extraction, STAIG uses the BYOL (Bootstrap Your Own Latent) self-supervised framework applied to bandpass-filtered image patches aligned to ST spots, avoiding the need for large histology-specific pretraining datasets — a limitation of prior approaches (stLearn, DeepST, ConST). An adjacency matrix is constructed from spatial distances between spots and combined diagonally across multiple slices for batch integration. The graph structure is dynamically adjusted during training: edges are probabilistically removed based on Euclidean distances between spots in the image embedding space (image-informed augmentation), and node features are randomly masked. A shared graph neural network (GNN) is trained with a neighborhood contrastive objective that aligns a node with its spatial neighbors in two augmented views while excluding homologous false-negative nodes using image-based prior knowledge (Debiased Strategy). For multi-slice integration, image embeddings are stacked vertically and adjacency matrices merged diagonally, enabling end-to-end batch integration without manual coordinate alignment. Benchmarking on 12 human dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) slices with manual cortical layer annotations (L1–L6 + WM) shows STAIG achieves the highest median ARI (0.69) and NMI (0.71) across all methods tested (Seurat, GraphST, DeepST, STAGATE, SpaGCN, SEDR, ConST, MuCoST, stLearn); best single-slice performance reaches ARI 0.84, NMI 0.78 (slice #151672). STAIG also successfully identifies fine-grained brain layers (MCL, IPL) in mouse olfactory bulb Stereo-seq data and accurately delineates tumor microenvironments in breast cancer H&E datasets, closely matching pathologist-annotated contours.
